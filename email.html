01 / TRAINING PHASE

Learn frequencies from 100 past emails

In the training phase, Naïve Bayes counts how often each category appears (the priors) and how often trigger words appear inside each category (the feature likelihoods).

02 / NEW EMAIL ARRIVES

Apply the Naïve Bayes formula

A brand-new email arrives. Toggle which trigger words appear in its text to test how the classifier evaluates the message:

Incoming email text
“Free Lottery”
P(Spam | "Free Lottery") = [ P(Free | Spam) × P(Lottery | Spam) × P(Spam) ] / P("Free Lottery")
P(Normal | "Free Lottery") = [ P(Free | Normal) × P(Lottery | Normal) × P(Normal) ] / P("Free Lottery")
03 / PREDICTION SCORES

Drop the shared denominator to compare

Since the denominator P(“Free Lottery”) is identical for both classes, we can omit it when comparing scores and multiply the prior by each word’s likelihood directly.

Score(Spam)
0.075
0.75 × 0.50 × 0.20 = 0.075
Score(Normal)
0.0005
0.05 × 0.0125 × 0.80 = 0.0005
04 / FINAL CLASSIFICATION

Normalize into posterior probabilities

Dividing each score by the sum of both scores converts them into percentages that add up to 100%.

P(Spam | "Free Lottery") = 0.075 / (0.075 + 0.0005) = 99.34%≈ 99.3% probability of being Spam

05 / WHY IT’S CALLED “NAÏVE”

Independent word evidence

Even though Normal emails outnumber Spam emails 4-to-1 overall (80% vs. 20%), “Free” is 15× more common in Spam (75% vs. 5%) and “Lottery” is 40× more common in Spam (50% vs. 1.25%). Multiplying those ratios overwhelms the prior.

Naïve Bayes gets its name because it assumes each word appears independently given the class—ignoring that “Free” and “Lottery” often appear together in scams. Despite that simplification, it works remarkably well in practice.

06 / PUTTING IT ALL TOGETHER

From Bayes’ theorem to real-world filters

This simple model scales to thousands of words at once: during training it stores word frequencies per class, and during prediction it multiplies priors and likelihoods to pick the class with the highest score.

The full progression

Bayes’ Theorem updates beliefs with new evidence.

MAP chooses the single most probable hypothesis.

Bayes Optimal combines all hypotheses weighted by posterior probability.

Naïve Bayes turns Bayesian classification into a fast, practical algorithm using conditional independence.
